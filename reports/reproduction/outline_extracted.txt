1. Match Predictability across Leagues  
We aim to evaluate what sets the top football leagues apart from the rest, and also to 
separate among the top leagues (e.g. Premier vs. rest). One theory is that a league is more 
exciting if the match results are less predictable. We perform the following analysis:  
1. Plan: 
Train classification models to predict the outcome of matches for selected leagues 
across different tiers, and see how accurate the predictions are. We train the 
models with match statistics of all matches from 2000/01 season to 2022/23, then 
try to predict the outcomes of games in each league from season 2023/24 to 
2025/26 . 
 
We used naive Bayes classification, logistic regression and histogram gradient 
boosting for our analysis.  
 
Results:

The Premier League is even slightly more predictable compared to other top tier 
football leagues. Therefore predictability does not contribute to the popularity of the 
Premier League. 
 
We further evaluated the predictability of games in less popular leagues. We picked 
15 leagues and grouped them into 3 tiers. The top tier includes Premier League (E0), 
La Liga (SP1), Bundesliga (D1), Serie A (I1) and Ligue 1 (F1), which were ranked 1-5 
by UEFA in 2023 . The mid tier includes Eredivisie (N1), Primeira Liga (P1), Pro League 
(B1), Premiership (SC0) and Bundesliga (AUT), which were the 6-10 th in the same 
UEFA ranking. The lower tier includes Allsvenskan (SWE), Ekstraklasa (POL), Liga I 
(ROM), Premier Division (IRL) and Veikkausliiga (FIN), which were ranked 20+ 
according to UEFA.

2. Each football match can have three outcomes: win, draw or loss. Thus the models 
are doing better than random guesses. However, the prediction accuracy was much 
lower than our expectation. We therefore thought to compare the results with the 
betting market rates and see which side better predicts match outcomes.

3. To improve the accuracy of our models, we trained another set of models with the
